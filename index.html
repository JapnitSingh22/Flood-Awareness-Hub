What is a Flood?

A flood is an overflow of water that submerges land that is usually dry. Floods can occur from heavy rainfall, storm surges, rapid snowmelt, or dam failures.

⚡

Flash Floods

Rapid flooding of low-lying areas in less than 6 hours. Often caused by intense rainfall or dam failure. These are extremely dangerous with little warning time.

🌊

River Floods

Occur when river levels rise and overflow their banks. Can develop slowly over days or weeks, providing more warning time for evacuation.

🌀

Coastal Floods

Caused by storm surges, hurricanes, or tsunamis. Coastal areas face rapid flooding when water levels rise quickly from ocean storms.

⚠️ Did You Know?

Flooding can occur even outside designated flood zones. Urban areas are particularly vulnerable due to reduced water absorption from concrete surfaces and storm drain systems.

Warning Signs of a Flood

🌧️

Heavy Rainfall

Intense or prolonged rainfall is the primary cause of flooding. Monitor weather forecasts and warnings from local authorities.

📻

Official Warnings

Pay attention to flood watches (possible flooding) and flood warnings (flooding is occurring or imminent). Act immediately on warnings.

⬆️

Rising Water Levels

Monitor nearby streams, rivers, and drainage channels. Water levels can rise rapidly, especially in flash flood situations.

❄️

Snowmelt & Ice Jams

Heavy spring rains on melting snow or ice jams breaking can cause sudden flooding. Be extra cautious during seasonal transitions.

🚨 Critical Warning

Turn Around, Don't Drown! Never walk, swim, or drive through floodwater. Just 6 inches of fast-moving water can knock you down, and 12 inches can carry away most vehicles.

Flood Safety Guidelines

🗺️

Know Your Risk

  • Check if you live in a flood-prone area
  • Learn your evacuation routes
  • Know your evacuation zone
  • Identify higher ground locations
📦

Emergency Kit

  • Water (1 gallon per person per day)
  • Non-perishable food (7-day supply)
  • First aid kit and medications
  • Battery-powered radio and flashlight
  • Important documents in waterproof container
🏠

Protect Your Home

  • Install check valves in sewer traps
  • Elevate electrical components
  • Clean gutters and downspouts
  • Consider flood insurance
  • Seal basement walls
👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family Plan

  • Create family communication plan
  • Designate meeting locations
  • Plan for pets and livestock
  • Register for emergency alerts
  • Practice evacuation routes
🏃

Evacuate Immediately

  • Follow evacuation orders without delay
  • Move to higher ground immediately
  • Don't wait for official instructions if in immediate danger
  • Take your emergency kit
🚗

Never Drive Through Water

  • Turn around if you encounter flooded roads
  • Don't drive around road barriers
  • If trapped in vehicle, move to roof
  • Abandon car if water is rising inside
⚡

Electrical Safety

  • Turn off electricity at main breaker
  • Never touch electrical equipment when wet
  • Assume downed power lines are live
  • Stay away from water near electrical sources
🏠

If Trapped at Home

  • Move to highest level (not attic without exit)
  • Signal for help if needed
  • Stay informed via battery radio
  • Don't walk through moving water
⏰

Wait for All-Clear

  • Only return when authorities say it's safe
  • Stay out of damaged buildings
  • Avoid floodwater and standing water
  • Watch for debris and hazards
🦠

Health Hazards

  • Floodwater may be contaminated
  • Wash hands frequently
  • Disinfect everything that got wet
  • Watch for mold growth
  • Seek medical attention for any wounds
📸

Document Damage

  • Take photos/videos of all damage
  • Contact insurance company
  • Make list of damaged items
  • Keep receipts for expenses
🔧

Cleanup Safety

  • Wear protective clothing and gear
  • Pump out water gradually (3ft per day)
  • Air out and dry property
  • Discard contaminated food
  • Listen to water safety advisories

Real-World Flood Case Studies

Learn from recent flood disasters and understand their impact, causes, and lessons learned.

📍

Punjab Floods - 2025

Date: August 2025

Affected Areas: Multiple districts including Mohali, Patiala, and Rupnagar

Cause: Excessive monsoon rainfall combined with overflow from the Sutlej River and breaches in drainage systems

Impact:

  • Over 100,000 people evacuated
  • Extensive crop damage across thousands of hectares
  • Major disruption to transportation networks
  • Damage to residential and commercial properties

Lessons Learned:

  • Importance of early warning systems
  • Need for improved drainage infrastructure
  • Critical role of timely evacuation
  • Community preparedness saves lives
📍

Kerala Floods - 2018

Date: August 2018

Affected Areas: 14 districts across Kerala state

Cause: Heaviest rainfall in nearly a century, combined with dam releases

Impact:

  • Over 400 lives lost
  • 1.4 million people displaced
  • Economic losses exceeding ₹40,000 crores
  • Complete submersion of multiple towns
  • Landslides in hilly regions

Lessons Learned:

  • Climate change increases extreme weather events
  • Deforestation and urbanization worsen flood impact
  • Coordinated rescue operations are vital
  • Social media can aid disaster response
  • Need for sustainable development practices
📍

Uttarakhand Flash Floods - 2013

Date: June 2013

Affected Areas: Kedarnath and surrounding Himalayan regions

Cause: Cloudburst and glacier lake bursts combined with massive rainfall

Impact:

  • Nearly 6,000 lives lost (presumed)
  • Over 100,000 pilgrims stranded
  • Complete destruction of Kedarnath town
  • Roads, bridges, and buildings washed away
  • Massive landslides blocked rivers

Lessons Learned:

  • Mountain regions face unique flood risks
  • Unplanned construction increases vulnerability
  • Flash floods provide minimal warning time
  • Rescue operations in remote areas are challenging
  • Environmental conservation is crucial

🎓 Key Takeaway from Case Studies

These disasters highlight the importance of preparation, early warning systems, and respecting nature. Climate change is making floods more frequent and severe. Communities must invest in infrastructure, maintain natural drainage systems, and have robust emergency response plans.
